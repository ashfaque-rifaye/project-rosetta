Project Rosetta turns "do your homework" into a family adventure on Fire TV.

Kids photograph their worksheet (or their room) with any phone. A crew of AI agents on Amazon Bedrock marks the work on the TV, with a hint for every mistake and never the answer, builds a practice round on exactly what slipped, and turns the week's learning into a co-op family quiz that lights up the Family Stone.

1 · The problem

Homework, chores and screen time are a nightly fight, and the TV is never part of the fix.

55% of familiesargue about screen time often or occasionally; 46% of parents struggle to enforce limits. AXA / Sotomo, 2025
"Have you done your homework?"Parents can't check every sum every night, and a child stuck on a mistake often just guesses again. Telling them the answer doesn't teach; leaving them alone doesn't either.
"Clean your room"is a vague, fight-starting instruction. Small concrete steps work, but parents rarely have the energy to break it down every night.
Tools add workParental controls only restrict. Chore apps make a parent approve every photo, which reviewers call burdensome.
The TV is the prizeEvery chore app lives on a phone. The TV just gets switched off as a punishment.
2 · How Rosetta solves it

Rosetta translates between parents and kids.

Like the Rosetta Stone let two languages understand each other, the app turns what parents say into something kids want to do.

"Do your homework."
→
Homework Check: every answer ticked or crossed on the TV, a hint for each mistake (never the answer), then practice on exactly the skills that slipped.
"I can't check all of this."
→
Trustworthy marking: the AI reads the handwriting; every sum is double-checked with exact maths in code before a mark is shown.
"Clean your room."
→
Concrete micro-quests: the exact out-of-place items are found in a photo and pinned on the TV as Lost Things.
"Is it done?"
→
Item-by-item proof: "4 of 5 home, just the jacket left," with a confidence score.
"No TV until…"
→
Progress, not punishment: every quest carves a glyph into the Family Stone; a full stone unlocks Movie Night.
"I'm tired of checking."
→
Autopilot: confident verdicts approve themselves; only unsure ones reach Mom or Dad as an alert.
"Stop fighting with your sister."
→
Co-op, never ranked: siblings play one Quiz Battle together, built from what each of them studied, and share the reward.
3 · Features

Everything a family sees, screen by screen.

Homework Check AI

Snap a worksheet; the Tutor agent marks each answer on the photo. Every sum is checked exactly in code. Wrong answers get a hint like "you added the bottom numbers too", never the answer.

Practice

Five fresh questions on exactly the skills that slipped, validated so there's one right option and never a repeat of the homework. Answer with the remote or the phone; each answer explains the method.

Room Scan → Lost Things

The kid snaps the room with a phone (QR from the TV). Scout finds what's out of place and each item becomes a stop-motion character pinned to the real object, with where it belongs.

Mission Control AI

A live graph of the agent crew: Quest Master routes work to Scout, Coach, Judge, Router and Keeper, with each step and confidence score shown as it happens.

Reward reveal

XP counts up, levels, coins, a new glyph, a passport stamp and Codex upgrades, revealed one beat at a time. Rewards are earned, never random.

Family Stone → Movie Night

Each finished quest carves a glyph. When the stone is complete, Movie Night unlocks for everyone. The Keeper reads it aloud.

Quiz Battle AI

The Quizmaster agent builds a family quiz from this week's homework, pitched at each child. Turns alternate; every right answer lights a glyph. The team total, never a ranking.

The Stone Trial Unity 6

The Quiz Battle in a real game engine: right answers ignite the family's carved glyphs with bloom and sparks. Opened from the TV with the quiz; answers sync back.

Codex, Passport, Shop

Collect every Lost Thing you rescue (rarity is earned by rescues), stamp a quest passport, and spend coins on outfits and decor that appear on the house.

Growth, for parents

Quests per day, tidy-time trend, self-starts and skill balance, with the Coach agent's plain-language notes on how habits are building.

Phones and parent alerts

Siblings send hype emotes that burst onto the TV. Mom and Dad get alerts by push, email or SMS: quest done, "please check", streak at risk, and a weekly digest. A parent PIN guards approvals.

4 · How it's built

Three places: the phones, the cloud, and the living room.

One homework quest, start to finish (live on AWS in about ten seconds)

  1. The TV opens the quest and shows a QR code. No kid accounts: a household is a join code like R4KX.
  2. The kid snaps the worksheet on any phone or Fire tablet. It goes straight to S3 and is deleted after one day.
  3. Quest Master wakes up. It's a Strands agent on Nova Pro that decides which specialist to call: here, the Tutor.
  4. Tutor reads and marks. Every question and handwritten answer is transcribed and marked; arithmetic is recomputed exactly in code and overrides the model.
  5. Confident? Practice begins. Unsure of the handwriting? Router asks Mom or Dad instead. Practice writes questions on the skills that slipped.
  6. The TV and phone show it all. Ticks and crosses on the photo, hints, the practice round, and Mission Control with every agent step.
  7. Rewards land everywhere. Reward reveal, a glyph on the Family Stone, a parent alert ("4/6 right, then practised adding fractions"), a data point in Growth, and material for Friday's Quiz Battle.

5 · How to run it

Start with the emulator. No AWS, no phone needed.

The TV app ships with a demo family (the Raos) and runs its household engine on the device, so everything works offline. Commands below are for Git Bash on this PC, from the project folder D:\Projects\AWS Hackathon - Amazon Build Ship Shape\Project Rosetta.

A. See the whole app on the emulator

Ready now · ~5 min
  1. Start the test TV emulator (it's on E:). Use ArenaTest_API36: it runs both the TV app and the Unity arena. The older FireTV_API30 runs only the TV app. 
    "$LOCALAPPDATA/Android/Sdk/emulator/emulator.exe" -avd ArenaTest_API36 -gpu swiftshader_indirect -memory 2048
  2. In a second Git Bash window, make adb easy to call for this session: 
    export PATH="$LOCALAPPDATA/Android/Sdk/platform-tools:$PATH"
  3. Install the TV app (Gradle builds it the first time, a few minutes): 
    cd tv-app && ./gradlew installDebug && cd ..
  4. Install the Unity arena (already built): 
    adb install -r "E:/Unity/Projects/RosettaArena/Builds/RosettaArena.apk"
  5. Open Rosetta: 
    adb shell am start -n app.rosetta.tv/.MainActivity

Prefer clicking? Open the tv-app folder in Android Studio, pick the running emulator in the device list and press Run. It does steps 3 and 5 for you.

Driving it like a TV remote

Arrow keys / D-padMove between quests, the raid card and the bottom bar. In the emulator, click its window first.
Enter / OKOpen the focused card.
Esc / BackGo back to Home Base. In the arena, return with your hits.
adb shell input keyevent KEYCODE_MENUStand-in for the kid's phone: sends the next room photo on a quest, or one put-away hit in a raid.
adb shell input keyevent KEYCODE_MEDIA_FAST_FORWARDStand-in for a sibling's phone: sends a hype emote to the TV.

A two-minute tour

  1. Open Fractions homework. The TV shows a QR code and waits for a phone.
  2. Send KEYCODE_MENU: the worksheet photo arrives, the Tutor marks it 4 of 6 (replaying the real Nova Pro output for that photo) and the practice round opens.
  3. Answer the five practice questions with the arrows and OK, then press OK on Claim rewards.
  4. Watch the reward reveal (Homework Hero glyph and stamp), then carve the glyph: the Family Stone is complete and Movie Night unlocks.
  5. Back on Home Base, press Up to The Stone Trial and OK. The Unity arena opens the family quiz; answer with the arrows and OK (MENU answers correctly). Press OK at the end to bring the result back to the TV.
  6. Try Rescue the Lost Things for the chores flow, and Codex, Passport, Shop and Growth from the bottom bar.

Ready now · 1 min

python web/dev_server.py

Then open http://localhost:8765/kid.html?code=R4KX and http://localhost:8765/parent.html?code=R4KX.

C. Run it on a real Fire TV Stick

Optional · needs the device
  1. On the Fire TV: Settings → My Fire TV → About, select the device name 7 times to unlock Developer Options. Then in Developer Options turn on ADB debugging and allow apps from unknown sources.
  2. Find its IP in Settings → My Fire TV → About → Network. Your PC must be on the same Wi-Fi.
  3. Connect and install both apps (accept the prompt on the TV the first time): 
    adb connect <fire-tv-ip>:5555
cd tv-app && ./gradlew installDebug && cd ..
adb install -r "E:/Unity/Projects/RosettaArena/Builds/RosettaArena.apk"
  4. Rosetta appears in Your Apps & Channels. The real remote's Menu (☰) key is the same stand-in for a phone.

D. Live mode with the real AI on AWS

Deployed · 22 Oct

The stack is live in your AWS account. API: https://jlgtsqwtzl.execute-api.us-east-1.amazonaws.com · phone page: https://dr8w3l96syzm7.cloudfront.net/kid.html?code=R4KX. To point the TV app at it, build with the API URL (last line below). To redeploy after changes, run the first three lines.

python assets/build_assets.py
python agents/build_lambda.py
cd infra && npm install && npx cdk deploy && cd ..
curl -X POST <ApiUrl>/household/R4KX/reset
cd tv-app && ./gradlew installDebug -ProsettaApiUrl=<ApiUrl> -ProsettaWebUrl=<WebUrl>

E. Rebuild the Unity arena after changes

Only if you edit it · ~15 min

Needs Unity 6 on E: and your signed-in Personal license. Close the emulator and Unity Hub first: low memory makes Unity's shader compiler crash.

bash arena-unity/sync.sh --build

The APK lands in E:/Unity/Projects/RosettaArena/Builds/RosettaArena.apk.

F. Run the backend tests

Ready now · 10 s
python -m unittest discover -s agents/tests

Twenty offline tests: the API, uploads, agent policy, parent alerts, the learning flow, exact maths and question validation, with AI calls stubbed.

6 · Outcome and impact

Where it stands today, and what it changes for a family.

Built and checked

  • Live on AWS: a real worksheet photo went phone → S3 → Strands Quest Master on Nova Pro → marked, with practice, in about ten seconds; practice answers and the parent alert worked end to end.
  • Homework marking: on our test sheets Nova alone agreed with the true marking on 10 of 11 answers; with the exact maths check, 11 of 11.
  • Fire TV app: Homework Check, Practice, Quiz Battle and every earlier screen run and screenshotted on the emulator.
  • Unity Stone Trial: launched from the TV with the family's quiz; answers came back to the TV.
  • Tests: 20 offline backend tests pass.
  • Open source: the VisionOverlay library with MIT license and README.

Not done yet

  • Parent phone page: the push sign-up, PIN and approve screens still need building on the page (the backend and alerts are deployed).
  • Email and SMS: deployed but need a verified SES sender and SNS SMS setup in your account before they can send.
  • TV in live mode: the TV app has been tested offline; a build pointed at the live API is the next check.
  • Real Fire TV hardware: not tested on a stick yet, only emulators.
  • Submission: final demo video, public GitHub repo and the Devpost form. Due Fri 23 Oct, 12:00 PT (Sat 00:30 IST).

For kids

Mistakes become hints and a short practice round instead of a red cross and a guess. Progress is visible, shared and never shamed.

For parents

Less checking: homework is marked reliably and practice is set automatically. A parent only answers the unsure cases, and gets a clear note of what was learned.

For siblings

Raids and the Family Stone are co-op. Nobody is ranked; the whole family wins Movie Night together.

For Fire TV

The TV stops being the prize that gets switched off and becomes the family's shared game board, with phones and tablets as controllers.

How impact would be measured. No families have used it yet, so there are no results to claim. The Growth screen already records what a pilot would track: quests per day, minutes to tidy, how often a kid starts without being asked, and how many verdicts needed a parent.
